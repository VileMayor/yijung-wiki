## Short Description

Koðak stand upright, are thick-chested and broad-shouldered, and continue to grow in girth and mass throughout their lives. Communities mark life by size, and many rites revolve around measuring one’s heart against increasing bulk.

## Biology & Physiology

### Age - Size Breakdown

- Newborn (0–1 year)
    - Height: 1.0–1.5 ft (30–45 cm)
    - Weight: 2–6 lb (1–3 kg)
    - Notes: puppy/polar-cub sized, extremely vulnerable.
- Juvenile (1–10 years)
    - Height: 2.5–4.5 ft (75–135 cm)
    - Weight: 20–120 lb (9–55 kg)
    - Notes: rapid height gain; still lithe.
- Young adult (10–40 years)
    - Height: 4.5–6.5 ft (135–200 cm)
    - Weight: 120–360 lb (55–163 kg)
    - Notes: many playable Kothak will come from this bracket.
- Adult (40–60 years)
    - Height: 6.5–8.0 ft (200–245 cm)
    - Weight: 300–650 lb (136–295 kg)
    - Notes: at 60 a Kothak will typically be starting to pass 8 ft (about 8.0–8.3 ft) and will often still be Medium.
- Mature (60–140 years)
    - Height: 8.0–9.0 ft (245–275 cm)
    - Weight: 600–1,000 lb (272–454 kg)
    - Notes: mass increases strongly here; cardiovascular strain becomes a real cultural/family concern.
- Elder / Terminal (140+ years)
    - Height: 9.0–10.0 ft (275–305 cm)
    - Weight: 900–1,400+ lb (408–635+ kg)
    - Notes: many natural deaths happen in this bracket as the heart finally can’t keep up; extremes vary by sex/niche.

## Variants & Subspecies

### Artic Koðak

- Ecology & subsistence
    - Diet focused on seals, whales, cold‑water fish and hardy tundra plants; hunts are large, cooperative, and seasonal.
    - Herds are highly migratory, tracking pack ice and marine mammal migrations.
- Technology & craft
    - Heavy insulating clothing from fur/blubber, sleds and drag sledges, bone/ivory carving, long‑term smokehouses and blubber‑rendering pits.
- Social behavior & politics
    - Matriarchs coordinate multi‑herd hunts and long‑range migrations; inter‑herd cooperation is essential.
    - Solitary males serve as long‑range scouts, ice patrols, and dangerous specialists; solitary success brings respect but limited reintegration.
- Rituals & symbolism
    - Ice cairns and memory mounds mark routes and sacred sites; elaborate sled or cairn burials; hibernation-style winter rituals.
- Interaction with outsiders
    - Less frequent contact; trade posts are strategic and politically sensitive.
    - More insular, conservative decision‑making; innovations adopted slowly and cautiously.
- Behavioral flavor
    - Stoic, long‑term planners who prioritize careful resource management and preservation of elders’ knowledge.

### Coastal Koðak

- Ecology & subsistence
    - Diet focused on fish, shellfish, crustaceans, kelp and occasional marine mammals; semi‑sedentary around productive coves and estuaries.
    - Seasonal moves are short and timed to spawning and tidal cycles.
- Technology & craft
    - Kelp weaving, driftwood construction, nets, skin‑boats, and ropework; lightweight, flexible gear adapted to water and rocky shorelines.
- Social behavior & politics
    - Matriarchs expert at reading tides and lunar/scent calendars; herds centered on reliable coves.
    - More frequent contact with outsiders (fisherfolk, traders, pirates) → pragmatism, diplomacy, and cultural exchange.
    - Bachelor bands often act as raiders, scavengers, or mercenaries in coastal raids.
- Rituals & symbolism
    - Sea‑rite offerings before mass harvests; kelp‑braiding as social symbol; water burials or sendings-off.
- Interaction with outsiders
    - Skilled sailors and traders: trade cured fish, kelp goods, and coastal knowledge.
    - More hybrid cultural practices due to regular contact.
- Behavioral flavor
    - Adaptive, mobile, opportunistic; matriarchs tend to be politically savvy and negotiators.
